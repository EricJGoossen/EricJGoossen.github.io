Our team of three met on October 23 to turn a whiteboard of ideas into a schedule for the tendon-driven arm. We split the work into two tracks that run side by side: mechanical design, built from the shoulder out, and the firmware that drives it, from a voltage controller up to impedance control. Each track has four one-week phases that end at our weekly meeting, from joints 1 and 2 through the forearm and wrist to the base, wire routing and parts list. Everything is scheduled to finish by November 20, which leaves four weeks of buffer before our December 18 target.

As the project lead, I wanted the plan to say what done means, not just what we're working on. Every phase has a definition of done that anyone on the team can check, such as range of motion verified in the assembly or the voltage loop's step response within spec. We also mapped the dependencies between tracks: the sensors chosen in the impedance plan decide what the wire routing has to carry. We run a lightweight agile hybrid of fixed milestones and one-week cycles. An item is done or not done, slips come out of the buffer, and post-MVP work like telemetry is cut before any date moves.
