Our robots know where they are from dead wheels: small unpowered tracking wheels with encoders that roll along the floor. When the engineer robot moved to new odometry pods with three of them, I [rewrote the team's Kalman-filter odometry](https://gitlab.com/aruw/controls/aruw-mcb/-/commit/57dd3eb62f9330f57eea4b856fb0c3f8f6fe5a7d) for them; I had taught myself Kalman filters from *Kalman Filter from the Ground Up*. The first version was one 8-state filter fusing the three wheels with the IMU. Its covariance between heading and the x and y motion turned out to be negligible, so I [split it](https://gitlab.com/aruw/controls/aruw-mcb/-/commit/32612de587558ed10e7d0453618395ba06c242d3) into three independent two-state filters, for x, y and heading, each updated by one measurement: every step now multiplies 2×2 matrices and divides by a number, instead of working through 8×8 matrices and inverting a 7×7 one, and the wheels alone turned out to be enough without the IMU. To tune it, I drove fixed paths (straight forward, straight sideways, spins and a square), logged the raw wheels, and replayed them offline to fit each pod's position, angle and wheel radius and the filter's noise.
