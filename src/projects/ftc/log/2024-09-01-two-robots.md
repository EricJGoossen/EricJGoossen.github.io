{% row "images/two-robot-plan.svg", "Timeline in two rows, software and mechanical: after the CAD and drive trains, software tests on robot A while mechanical builds the lift on robot B; at each definition of done the two teams trade robots, so software takes the robot with the newest feature and mechanical builds the next feature on the other", { caption: "The hand-off: at each definition of done, the two teams trade robots." } %}
As captain I set up a new way to build our robot: two robots developed in parallel. While the CAD was being finished, we assembled the drive trains. Software then got the first robot, a bare drive train, to test on, while mechanical integrated the first feature, such as the lift, on the second. Once that feature passed our definition of done, the teams traded: software integrated the feature and kept developing, and mechanical moved on to the next feature, like the arm, on the other robot, and the trading continued all season. Software was always testing on a competition-ready robot while mechanical kept building, our drivers got more practice before the robot was feature complete, and we would have had a robot ready for competition even if mechanical badly missed a deadline.
