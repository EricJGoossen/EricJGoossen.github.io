---
title: "SIMD: eight robots per instruction"
---
{# Placeholder: Eric to confirm this paragraph (written from his memviz measurements). #}
V3 stores every field as a structure of arrays: each component of each field, say the x angular velocity of every joint, is its own contiguous, aligned array across all the robots. So one instruction can load, update or store the same quantity for eight robots at once (eight lanes with AVX2; the default SSE build does four), and independent robots step in lockstep, one per SIMD lane. To check that the compiled binary really does this, I recorded every load and store of one step with Valgrind (eight three-joint arms): **88.9% of all accesses to simulation data are whole, aligned 8-lane vector loads or stores**, and in the velocity integrator every access to joint velocity and acceleration is a 32-byte, 8-float vector (126 of 126 aligned). The trace also showed where the remaining cost lives: about 80% of all accesses go to the stack, because a 6×6 matrix of batches is 36 registers' worth and AVX2 has 16.
