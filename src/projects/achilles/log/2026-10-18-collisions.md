The design doc planned cuRobo-style sphere colliders. What I built is more general: every body is a sphere-swept box, so zero extents make a sphere, one extent a capsule and zero radius a plain box. A broad phase tests swept-box world bounds and writes a bitmask of candidate pairs, and a MuJoCo/MJX-style narrow phase turns each candidate into up to four contacts with a normal, a signed distance and a point, computed across SIMD lanes like everything else. Against an exact signed-distance calculation on 1,800 random sphere, capsule and box pairs, **every distance Achilles reported agreed to within 5×10⁻⁵** (median 2×10⁻⁸). MuJoCo, on the same pairs, matched for spheres and capsules, but its box colliders, which approximate some cases, were off on 86.
{% endrow %}

{% row "images/contact-solver.mp4", "A blocky humanoid robot with gray torso and orange limbs tips over sideways, lands on a flat ground plane and lies on it, its arms still swinging", { poster: "images/contact-solver.jpg", caption: "Half speed. Contacts only matter once something acts on them, so next came the contact solver: MuJoCo's soft-constraint solver (primal conjugate gradient, pyramidal friction cones), run in MuJoCo's own step order. ABA computes the unconstrained accelerations, the broad and narrow phases find this pose's contacts, and the solver replaces ABA's accelerations with constrained ones before the integrator uses them. The demo is a passive humanoid on a ground plane: capsule limbs, a rounded-box torso and head, a free-floating torso joint, and nothing to hold it up, so it topples and lands on the plane instead of falling through it.
