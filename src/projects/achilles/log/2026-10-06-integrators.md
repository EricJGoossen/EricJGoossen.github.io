With the accelerations right, longer runs exposed the next problem: semi-implicit Euler drifts. A frictionless double pendulum should conserve energy, yet over 10 seconds at a 2 ms step Euler's total energy wanders by up to 0.56 J. Euler updates the velocity from the current acceleration, then moves the position with that new velocity, which is simple but only first-order accurate. Velocity Verlet splits the velocity update into two half-steps around the position update and recomputes the accelerations in between, halving the drift to 0.28 J. Its weakness is that velocity-dependent forces like Coriolis are evaluated at an estimated velocity. Implicit midpoint evaluates the dynamics at the average of the old and new states, making each step time-symmetric. Because the new state isn't known in advance, it iterates four times to converge, and it stays within 0.0006 J. RK4 instead samples the dynamics four times across the step and blends the results, reaching fourth-order accuracy and staying within 0.0001 J.
