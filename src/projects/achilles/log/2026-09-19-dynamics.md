V2's dynamics were wrong in ways only a reference could show: brought back to life next to MuJoCo, a one-link arm swung exactly 4× too fast (it used the free-body inverse inertia, dropping the parallel-axis term), and a two-link arm moved in the wrong shape. V3 runs the full Articulated Body Algorithm, with Coriolis and centrifugal bias terms, parent-to-body transforms and gravity as a fictitious base acceleration, and steps it forward with semi-implicit Euler, the same default MuJoCo uses. Here all three drive the same arm with the same shoulder torque and no gravity, since V2 never had any. **V3 stays within 3×10⁻⁵ rad of MuJoCo for all 4 seconds**, while V2 has swung the shoulder 4.5 times as far and folded the elbow forward instead of letting it trail behind.
{% endrow %}

{% row "images/dp-accel-error.svg", "Log-scale chart over 10 seconds of a double pendulum: the worst relative error in Achilles' joint accelerations against the closed-form solution stays between about 2e-7 and 3e-6, just above the float32 precision line, with no growth over time", { caption: "ABA against the closed-form solution at every state of a 10-second double-pendulum run." } %}
MuJoCo is only a reference, so I also checked V3 against the exact answer. On a double pendulum under gravity, its joint accelerations match the closed-form Lagrangian equations of motion to within 3×10⁻⁶ at every state of a 10-second run, which is float32 round-off, and match MuJoCo to the same 3×10⁻⁶.
