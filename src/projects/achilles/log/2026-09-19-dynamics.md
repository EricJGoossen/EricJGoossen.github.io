{% row "images/dp-accel-error.svg", "Log-scale chart over 10 seconds of a double pendulum: the worst relative error in Achilles' joint accelerations against the closed-form solution stays between about 2e-7 and 3e-6, just above the float32 precision line, with no growth over time", { caption: "ABA against the closed-form solution at every state of a 10-second double-pendulum run." } %}
V2's dynamics were wrong in ways only a reference could show: brought back to life next to MuJoCo, a one-link arm swung exactly 4× too fast (it used the free-body inverse inertia, dropping the parallel-axis term), and a two-link arm moved in the wrong shape. V3 runs the full Articulated Body Algorithm, with Coriolis and centrifugal bias terms, parent-to-body transforms and gravity as a fictitious base acceleration. On a double pendulum, its joint accelerations match the closed-form Lagrangian equations of motion to within 3×10⁻⁶, which is float32 round-off, and match MuJoCo to the same 3×10⁻⁶.
{% endrow %}

{% row "images/dp-energy.svg", "Chart of the change in total energy over 10 seconds for four integrators: semi-implicit Euler wanders between about −0.56 and +0.49 joules, velocity Verlet between about −0.28 and +0.24, and implicit midpoint and RK4 stay flat on the zero line", { caption: "Energy should stay constant. Double pendulum released from horizontal, 2 ms step." } %}
With the dynamics right, the integrator decides how long a simulation stays physical, so I built four: semi-implicit Euler (the library default, like MuJoCo's), velocity Verlet, implicit midpoint and RK4. Over 10 seconds of a double pendulum at a 2 ms step, Euler's total energy wanders by up to 0.56 J and Verlet's by 0.28 J, while implicit midpoint stays within 0.0006 J and RK4 within 0.0001 J, at the price of four dynamics evaluations per step.
{% endrow %}
