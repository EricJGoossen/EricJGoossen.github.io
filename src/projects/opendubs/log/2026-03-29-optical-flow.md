The base needed to know where it was before it could navigate, and the first source was optical flow: a camera-like sensor that looks at the floor and measures how fast it slides past. I first brought up SparkFun's Optical Tracking Odometry Sensor on an M5Stack board, publishing its pose into ROS over micro-ROS, then fed the flow into ArduPilot, whose EKF fuses it with the Pixhawk's IMU. The sensor only gave sensible readings within about 10 cm of the floor, so it had to be mounted low, on a longer cable than it came with, and it needed its own light: I added an LED under the robot with a ROS node to set its brightness, and found the flow quality stopped improving at about 12 lumens.
{% endrow %}

It sits near 0.1 m while the robot is parked, stays under 0.74 m while it drives 5.5 meters, and after the robot spins in place it climbs past 100 m to a peak of 155 m", { caption: "One recorded run from March 29: ArduPilot's estimate against motion capture, after fitting the two frames together over the first two minutes." } %}
To know how good that estimate was, I compared it against the lab's motion capture system. I wrote the ROS package that reads mocap over VRPN, converts the room's y-up frame to the robot's z-up one, offsets the marker frame to the robot's center and publishes odometry with filtered velocity, so the two could be logged side by side in one bag. The first recordings showed exactly why that mattered. **While the robot drove 5.5 meters, the estimate stayed within 0.74 m of the truth, but when it spun in place, the estimate ran away to 155 m** while the robot itself moved less than half a meter. My teammates traced it to two things: the sensor was mounted 90° off from the flight controller without that being in the configuration, and ArduPilot treats flow faster than about 4 rad/s as untrustworthy, which at the sensor's height is only about 20 cm/s.
{% endrow %}

From there, the work was tuning against mocap. I added scripts that plot the sensor's reported quality and height over a run and compute absolute and relative pose error against mocap with evo for any recorded bag, and used them to tune the flow sensor's gain. Once the full system was together, the error came down to about 5 mm. {# Placeholder: 5 mm over what? #}
