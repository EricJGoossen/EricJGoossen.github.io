mj_manipulator, the lab's MuJoCo manipulation library, planned for one arm at a time. With two arms on one torso that isn't safe: each arm's plan is collision-free on its own, and then the two plans hit each other. I rebuilt planning around an `ArmGroup`, which concatenates any number of arms' joints and plans in that combined space, so every step is checked arm against arm as well as against the world. Goals are keyed by arm name, and arms left out of a goal hold still. On the test case in the clip, where straight-line motion puts the arms through each other in 467 of 2,798 steps, **ArmGroup's plan reaches the same goals with zero contact at every step**, and finds it in 1.4 seconds.
{% endrow %}

The same group plans to joint configurations, gripper poses (solving IK per arm and checking the combinations together), task space regions, and Cartesian paths that all arms step through in lockstep. A goal one arm can't reach fails the whole plan instead of quietly moving only the other arm. Planning keeps a 5 mm safety margin between all geometry, and the retimed trajectory is collision-checked again at every sample, densifying and retrying if retiming cut a corner, with joint acceleration limits enforced. Single-arm code wraps its arm in a one-arm group, so there's one planning path for everything. Test suites written alongside it plan arms through each other's workspace, check that a fixed seed reproduces the same trajectory, that bad goals fail loudly, and that an arm without a goal doesn't drift at any point along the way.
