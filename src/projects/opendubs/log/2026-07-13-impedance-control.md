Each arm is a 7-DOF OpenArm whose Damiao motors run their own impedance loop: the Thor sends position, velocity and torque targets with gains over CAN-FD, and each motor closes the loop itself. I measured the stock ROS 2 stack first. A goal is accepted in 2.55 ms, and with both arms holding one block, **47 ms passes between a command to one arm and the torque spike in the other** (σ = 4.7 ms).

I wrote three impedance controllers with Pinocchio ([Cartesian](https://github.com/EricJGoossen/openarm_cartesian_control), [joint and null space](https://github.com/EricJGoossen/openarm_impedance_control)). Cartesian impedance left the redundant elbow free to drift off the collision-checked path; adding joint impedance fought it numerically; a null-space term worked but added gains to tune. What runs now is simpler: the planned trajectory goes straight to the motors, plus gravity compensation from Pinocchio at 500 Hz, with every goal checked against joint, workspace and torque limits.
