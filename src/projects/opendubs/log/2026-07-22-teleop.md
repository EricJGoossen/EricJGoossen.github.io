To give the arms demonstrations and a way to drive them in real time, I built teleoperation with a Meta Quest. Earlier pose sources needed an app installed on the headset, so I replaced them with a page served from the robot: the Quest's own browser opens it over HTTPS, enters AR, and streams both controllers' poses, grip and trigger over a websocket. The bridge has the same functions as the SDK it replaced, so switching sources was a one-line import change, and any other browser on the network that opens the page becomes a live diagnostics view of what the headset is sending.

Squeezing a controller's grip engages its arm, so the arm follows the hand only while it's held, and the trigger opens and closes the gripper, with a hysteresis band so one squeeze can't register twice. I built it against the arms in MuJoCo first, then added the real arms the next day. Getting it to feel right was mostly about speed: a cap on how far the gripper target can move per tick and on its Cartesian speed, a configurable loop rate, a switch between running the loop synchronously and asynchronously, and gripper commands that no longer block the loop while the gripper moves. {# Placeholder: the speed issues and the numbers #}

Along the way, the analytical IK solver failed on the OpenArm's geometry. I wrote scripts to sweep and correlate the failures, traced them to the arm's zero-length links, which make the solver's equations degenerate, and patched the solver to nudge those lengths by a tiny fraction of the arm's scale. {# Placeholder: "from failing to 50/50 on library test cases", and upstream status #}
